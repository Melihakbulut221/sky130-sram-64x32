github.com/Melihakbulut221/sky130-sram-64x32

This is a six transistor SRAM: 64 words of 32 bits, on SkyWater's open source 130 nm process, SKY130.

The cell array is custom layout, generated by Python code, and everything is checked with open tools.

Each bit lives in two inverters connected in a loop, and two pass gates connect it to the bit lines.

Sizing the cell is a tug of war.

The pull down must beat the pass gate, or a read flips the cell.

And the pass gate must beat the pull up, or a write fails.

I chose the sizes from butterfly curves over all process, voltage and temperature corners.

The worst case read margin is 218 millivolts, and a thousand Monte Carlo samples per corner show no failures.

The cell is drawn with the ordinary logic design rules, so the standard rule decks apply without any waivers.

That costs area: it is about twice the size of the foundry's own cell.

Mirrored neighbours share their contacts.

Sixty four rows of thirty two cells make the array: 12,288 transistors.

Around the array there is no sense amplifier, and no self timed pulse.

The bit lines swing from rail to rail, and a plain inverter senses them.

The bit lines precharge while the clock is high, and the access happens while it is low.

If a phase is too short, you slow the clock down.

That periphery is ordinary Verilog, placed and routed as standard cells next to the array.

The complete memory measures 225 by 156 µm.

The final layout has zero design rule violations in two independent checkers, and it matches its netlist transistor by transistor.

In SPICE on the extracted layout, a word is written and read back.

The data is out 2.5 nanoseconds after the clock edge, and 5.0 nanoseconds at the slow corner.

It comes with timing libraries, a simulation model and a self test.

What it does not have yet is silicon: nothing here has been fabricated.
